What does a corporate lawyer do?

A corporate lawyer sets up your business correctly and keeps it compliant: SECP company registration, NTN and sales tax registration with the FBR, annual tax returns, trademark registration with IPO Pakistan, and the contracts your business runs on. MeraLawyer.pk handles the filing for you and quotes a fixed fee upfront.

Key facts at a glance

  • Companies in Pakistan are incorporated with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) under the Companies Act 2017.
  • A single member company or private limited company can be registered online through SECP's e-services.
  • Businesses register for income tax with FBR; sales tax on goods is with FBR, while sales tax on services is with FBR for Islamabad and the Punjab Revenue Authority for Rawalpindi.
  • Trademarks are registered with the Intellectual Property Organization of Pakistan under the Trade Marks Ordinance 2001.
  • Registered companies must file annual returns and keep statutory records; missed filings can lead to penalties.

What does a corporate lawyer do for a business in Islamabad or Rawalpindi?

A corporate lawyer helps you set up the right business structure, register it, and keep it compliant. For a new business that usually means choosing between a sole proprietorship, a partnership, a single member company or a private limited company, registering with SECP and FBR, and putting basic contracts in place. For an existing business it means annual filings, shareholder changes, contracts, employment documents and disputes.

Good corporate advice is preventive. Clear shareholder agreements, properly drafted contracts and timely filings avoid most of the disputes and penalties that small businesses in the twin cities run into.

Which business structure should you choose?

The right structure depends on liability, the number of owners, the need to raise investment and how clients or banks view the business. Your lawyer will explain the trade-offs, but the main options are summarised below.

  • Sole proprietorship: simplest to start, registered for tax with FBR; the owner is personally liable
  • Partnership: two or more partners; can be registered with the Registrar of Firms; partners share liability
  • Single member company (SMC): one owner with limited liability, registered with SECP
  • Private limited company: two or more members with limited liability; preferred for investment and contracts
  • Limited liability partnership (LLP): registered with SECP; combines partnership flexibility with limited liability

How is a company registered with SECP?

Registration starts with reserving a company name on SECP's online system, followed by filing the incorporation documents, including the memorandum and articles of association and the details of directors and subscribers. Once SECP approves the application, it issues a certificate of incorporation. Through integration between SECP and FBR, the company also receives its National Tax Number.

After incorporation, the company should open a bank account, register for sales tax if required, and set up its statutory registers. Your lawyer can also register the company with the relevant chamber of commerce in Islamabad or Rawalpindi if you need membership for trade or tenders.

What ongoing compliance does a company need?

Incorporation is the start, not the end. Companies that miss filings face penalties and can find it hard to open accounts, bid for tenders or attract investors.

  • Annual return filing with SECP and holding the annual general meeting where required
  • Filing changes of directors, shareholding or registered office with SECP
  • Annual income tax returns and monthly or quarterly withholding statements with FBR
  • Monthly sales tax returns if registered with FBR or PRA
  • Maintaining statutory registers and minutes of meetings

Which contracts should every business have?

Most business disputes come from verbal deals or copied templates. A corporate lawyer drafts contracts that reflect Pakistani law and your actual arrangement, including payment terms, termination, dispute resolution and confidentiality.

  • Shareholder or partnership agreement
  • Service and supply agreements with clients and vendors
  • Employment contracts and non-disclosure agreements
  • Commercial rent agreement for your office or shop
  • Software, franchise or distribution agreements where relevant

Can foreign or overseas investors register a company in Pakistan?

Yes. Foreign nationals and overseas Pakistanis can be directors and shareholders of a Pakistani company. Some additional documents and security clearance may be required for foreign directors, and certain sectors have special rules. A corporate lawyer handles the process remotely, with documents attested where needed.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lawyer to register a company?

It is not legally required, but a lawyer or corporate consultant ensures your memorandum and articles of association, shareholding and filings are correct, which prevents problems with banks, FBR and investors later.

Is a sole proprietorship or a private limited company better?

A sole proprietorship is cheaper and faster to set up but you are personally liable for its debts. A private limited company separates your personal assets from the business and is preferred by banks, investors and large clients.

When is the income tax return due in Pakistan?

For individuals and AOPs the due date is usually 30 September each year for the previous tax year, unless the FBR announces an extension.
