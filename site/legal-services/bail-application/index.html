Quick answer: bail application

Bail is applied for under section 497 (post-arrest) or section 498 (pre-arrest) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, usually first before the Sessions Court and then, if refused, before the Islamabad High Court or the Lahore High Court Rawalpindi Bench. The lawyer files the petition with the FIR copy and argues why the accused should be released or protected from arrest.

Key facts at a glance

  • Pre-arrest bail is sought under section 498 CrPC; post-arrest bail in non-bailable offences under section 497 CrPC.
  • In bailable offences, bail is a right under section 496 CrPC.
  • A bail application is normally filed in the Court of Session first, and in the High Court if refused.
  • Courts consider the nature of the offence, the evidence, whether the case needs further inquiry, and the conduct of the accused.
  • Bail granted can be cancelled if its conditions are misused.

Who needs bail application?

Anyone named in an FIR, a person already arrested, or their family members acting for them.

When do you need to file a bail application?

You need a bail application when an FIR has been registered against you or a family member in a non-bailable offence. If the person has not yet been arrested and fears arrest, the application is for pre-arrest bail. If the person is already in custody, the application is for post-arrest bail. Timing matters: the sooner a lawyer reviews the FIR and the police record, the stronger and faster the application can be.

  • An FIR has been registered and arrest is likely
  • A family member has been arrested and is on physical or judicial remand
  • Bail was refused by the sessions court and you want to approach the High Court
  • You live abroad and need protective bail to return and join the investigation

What are the grounds for bail?

Courts decide each application on its facts, but certain grounds come up often. In post-arrest bail, the defence may argue that the case calls for further inquiry under section 497(2), that the accused is not required for investigation, that the offence does not fall within the prohibitory clause, or that there has been delay in trial. Special considerations apply to women, minors and sick or elderly accused.

In pre-arrest bail, the court additionally looks for signs that the case is motivated by malice or an ulterior purpose, for example a civil dispute turned into a criminal case. Evidence supporting this, such as documents of an earlier dispute, is very helpful.

How does the bail process work in Islamabad and Rawalpindi?

Your lawyer collects the FIR and any available record, drafts the application with supporting documents, and files it before the competent court. In Islamabad, that is usually the Court of Session in the district courts, and then the Islamabad High Court. In Rawalpindi, the district courts at Kachehri and then the Lahore High Court, Rawalpindi Bench. Cases under special laws go to the relevant special court.

The court issues notice to the prosecution and the complainant, calls the police record, and hears arguments. In pre-arrest bail, the court may grant interim bail until the next hearing on the condition that the accused joins the investigation. If bail is granted, the accused furnishes surety bonds as directed by the court.

What documents are needed for a bail application?

Bring what you have; the lawyer will obtain the rest.

  • Copy of the FIR and details of the police station
  • CNIC of the accused and of the proposed surety
  • Any documents showing an earlier civil or family dispute
  • Medical records if health is a ground
  • Previous orders, if bail was refused in a lower court
  • Property or salary documents of the surety, if required by the court

How long does it take to get bail?

There is no fixed timeline. Interim pre-arrest bail can sometimes be obtained on the day of filing if the application is moved urgently. Post-arrest bail usually takes longer because the court calls the police record. Delays also depend on the court's workload, adjournments requested by the other side, and how complete the application is.

What happens after bail is granted?

The accused must attend every trial hearing, cooperate with the investigation if required, and not threaten witnesses or tamper with evidence. Breaching these conditions can lead to cancellation of bail under section 497(5). Bail does not end the case; the trial continues, and your lawyer should plan the defence from the start.

Bail application: frequently asked questions

Which court do I apply to for bail?

Usually the Sessions Court first. If bail is refused, you can apply to the High Court.

Is bail available in non-bailable offences?

Yes, bail can be granted in non-bailable offences at the court's discretion. It is harder in offences carrying severe punishment.

What is a surety?

A person who guarantees the accused will attend court, usually by submitting a bond backed by property or income documents.
